# AFTERPARTY — Inspiration Brief (scouted 2026-09-29)

Rooftop afterparty. One guest is the **Echo** (uninvited). A living **Muse** GM rewrites every round. Single-device, hot-seat, browser. Steal from 2024–2026 party/deduction hits; ship tonight, not a 40-role Clocktower.

Sources in play: Jackbox Pack 11 (Suspectives, Hear Say, Doominate, Cookie Haus, Legends of Trivia), Blood on the Clocktower, Fake Artist Goes to New York, Wavelength, Resistance/Avalon, Coup, Spyfall, Drawful/Fibbage/Quiplash, Trivia Murder Party, One Night Ultimate Werewolf, Gartic Phone, Codenames, Kahoot, Mario Party, The Chameleon, Just One, Herd Mentality.

---

## 1. Eight mechanics to steal / remix tonight

### 1. Ghosts stay in the room
**Source:** Blood on the Clocktower — death is not a sit-out. Dead players still talk; they keep one “ghost vote” that gets louder as the table shrinks. Roles stay hidden on death so the puzzle doesn’t collapse.

**AFTERPARTY tonight:** If a guest is “caught,” “kicked off the roof,” or “sent for ice,” they become a **voicemail / hangover ghost**. They still roast, accuse, and get **one last vote**. Never a spectator. Aim is team-win, not staying alive. Muse never reveals the Echo on a kick.

### 2. Your answers become the evidence
**Source:** Jackbox Pack 11 *Suspectives* (10–15 min). Icebreaker survey first; those answers become crime clues. One clue is **phony**. Interrogation + lie-detector mid-game. Survey questions must be: fast to answer, broad enough not to out anyone immediately, narrow enough to spark opinions.

**AFTERPARTY tonight:** Opening 6–8 rooftop questions (“last song you actually know,” “would you steal the aux,” “hat tonight: yes/no”). After the party “crime” (spilled punch, stolen playlist, missing plus-one), Muse reveals clues as **guest survey answers**. Echo must blend; table pins who the clue fits. One clue is a fake planted by a non-Echo. Two-vote final suspects.

### 3. One stroke, one fake
**Source:** *A Fake Artist Goes to New York* (Wirecutter 2025/26 staple). Everyone knows the title except one. Shared drawing, **one contiguous line per turn**, two laps, then point. If caught, fake still wins by guessing the title. Real artists must draw *just enough* — too clear and the fake steals it.

**AFTERPARTY tonight:** **Guest-book mural** on the TV. Category is public (“rooftop snacks”), title is secret, Echo draws blind. Pass-the-device for each stroke (or Muse draws the line the hot-seat player traces with a finger). Vote the faker; if Echo is named they get one guess at the title. Tight: two laps, 8 seconds a stroke.

### 4. Calibrate the room, not the fact
**Source:** Wavelength — still called the best pure party design of the last decade. Hidden target on a subjective spectrum; one clue; team argues where *this person* thinks it sits. Other team still plays (left/right intercept). Debates are the game.

**AFTERPARTY tonight:** Muse slaps a vibe bar on screen (`this party is a nap ←→ a riot`, `innocent guest ←→ definitely the Echo`). Clue-giver is hot-seat. Room shouts a number 0–100 or drags a shared needle. Echo secretly tries to **land off-center** or **perfectly mimic** the group’s taste. Scoring is closeness, not trivia. 60-second argument cap.

### 5. Missions, not murders
**Source:** The Resistance / Avalon. No elimination. Propose a team, table votes, secret success/fail. Spies know each other; Merlin knows spies but must not be clocked. Five rejected teams = evil win. Everyone plays every round.

**AFTERPARTY tonight:** “Who goes to the kitchen / DJ booth / fire escape?” Leader nominates 2–3 guests. Table thumbs up/down. On the run, Echo may **sabotage** (one fail token). Three sabotages vs three clean runs. Optional **Muse-who-knows** (one guest sees the Echo but must hint without outing themselves — Avalon’s Merlin). Nobody sits.

### 6. Claim it, or get called
**Source:** Coup. Lie freely. Anyone can challenge a character claim: prove it or lose a life. Challenge is priced (wrong call costs you). 60-second teach. Bluff early when lies are cheap.

**AFTERPARTY tonight:** Guests claim **party powers** with no proof: “I saw the Echo refill a cup,” “I have the aux,” “I can clear a name.” Anyone can **call it**. Muse confirms true/false instantly. Failed claim = a strike (or you drink the bad punch). Failed call = *you* take the strike. Fast, loud, no homework.

### 7. You might not be who you think
**Source:** One Night Ultimate Werewolf. One night, one day, one vote. Robber/Troublemaker/Drunk **swap roles** so players can be wrong about themselves. ~10 minutes. App-timed night. No multi-night slog.

**AFTERPARTY tonight:** 20-second “lights down” (device face-down). Muse narrates two swaps: drinks switched, names on cups swapped, “you’re covering for someone.” Then 90 seconds of talk and one simultaneous point. Echo win if they aren’t the plurality. Perfect as a **palette-cleanse round** between heavier ones.

### 8. Score the truth *and* the trap
**Source:** Drawful / Fibbage / Quiplash. Dual purse: points for the real answer **and** for luring friends onto your fake. Drawful: weird prompt → draw → fake titles → vote. Quiplash: two answers, room votes, final “everyone writes the same prompt.” Jackbox: one task at a time; skippable tutorial.

**AFTERPARTY tonight:** Hot-seat draws or one-lines a rooftop moment. Rest of the room writes **fake captions** on a pass or shouted into Muse. Vote the real one. Artist scores on correct guesses; fakes score on bait. Gallery reveal is the joke, not the scoring screen.

## 2. Six Muse twists (make the round feel alive)

Muse is Clocktower’s Storyteller + Jackbox’s host: rewrites the *script*, not the physics. Use **at most one twist per round**. Announce it in one sentence.

1. **Phony clue.** After three pieces of evidence, Muse admits one is fake (Suspectives lie-detector). Table votes which to burn. If they burn a real one, Muse reveals one player’s honest survey answer. If they burn the fake, Echo sweats.

2. **Soft info, never hard out.** Steal BOTC’s “one of these two is wrong,” never “you are the Echo.” Muse says: “The Echo laughed at that last joke” / “The Echo did **not** draw the circle.” Always leaves an alibi. Stops toxic certainty; keeps the puzzle.

3. **Mid-round rewrite.** If the table has already solved it, Muse **changes the win condition** in one line: “Actually, tonight the Echo wins if they get *named* and then correctly name the song.” Or swaps two secret roles (ONUW Troublemaker) without saying who. Do this when energy dips, not as a gloat.

4. **Name the table meta.** Clocktower storytellers win by narrating *this group*. Muse: “You’re all going to be shocked, because [Player] is never the Echo… except the cup they just claimed is empty.” Treat recurring bits as lore. The GM is a character at the party, not a rules PDF.

5. **Dead-air inject.** If a vote stalls past 15s of silence, Muse **drops a 20-second minigame** (TMP Killing Floor energy, but no sit-out): rapid Kahoot sting, “pass the aux” clap, or a one-stroke doodle. Loser doesn’t leave — they become the next hot-seat or gain a strike. The room never waits on a thinker.

6. **Spotlight trial.** One player on the couch, 30 seconds to defend, room holds a “innocent / sus” meter (Suspectives interrogation). Muse asks **one** question tailored to last round (“Why did you vote for the mural circle?”). Then instant verdict sting. Next hot-seat is the accused *or* their accuser.

## 3. Anti-patterns (do not ship these)

**Elimination boredom.** Classic Werewolf/Mafia/Among Us ejection is the genre’s original sin. First-out sits 20 minutes. TMP 1–2 even made *ghosts* still trivia; TMP3 moved toward threads/revives because sit-out is poison. AFTERPARTY: kicked guests become ghosts with a job (vote, caption, minigame). If you must “kill,” kill a **token**, not a person.

**Too much reading.** Role walls, night-order sheets, 90-second tutorials. Jackbox’s own Pack 11 patch added **bypass tutorial** and “start at round two.” Jack Principles: one task, few choices, user always knows what to do. Teach in the first action, not a slide. Muse speaks **one line of rules** then play. No paragraph on a phone nobody can pass around.

**Dead air.** Waiting on a slow typer, a closed-eye night phase, or “discuss until someone calls vote.” Pack 11 games are 15–20 min for a reason. Caps: 8s draw stroke, 15s caption, 60s debate, 10s vote. If input doesn’t come, **move on** (Jack Principle 8). Show a waiting pulse on screen so the room feels the clock. Hot-seat typing is the #1 killer — prefer taps, sliders, two-button claims.

**Hard accusations.** BOTC specifically designed “one of you is evil, not which” because “I know you’re the demon” makes new players freeze. Muse and UI should frame **pairs and patterns**, not gotchas.

**Simultaneous secret input on one device.** This is not jackbox.tv. Do not design eight private surveys at once unless you have a **hide-and-pass ritual** (device face-down, Muse covers, 5s each). Default to public play + one private peek.

**Over-scripted nights.** Unique powers for every guest is Clocktower at 7+ with a human storyteller. Tonight: **one Echo, maybe one Muse-who-knows, everyone else is a guest**. Variety comes from round *type*, not a 12-role grimoire.

**Trivia as the whole game.** Kahoot/TMP work because stakes and comedy sit on top of the question. A rooftop quiz with no Echo, no fake, no sabotage is a classroom.

## 4. Juice / feel — single-device + hot-seat web

This is a **TV + one phone/laptop** game. The star is the couch, not the cursor.

**Jack Principles (still the bible):**
- One task at a time. Never “draw AND vote AND type a name.”
- Meaningful choices only (2–4 taps).
- Make the program **feel like it’s waiting** (pulse, Muse tap-foot line, shrinking ring).
- If they stall, the Muse moves on and teases them.
- Host talks like a person in the room. Never repeat the same line.

**Hot-seat ceremony**
- Big “PASS TO ___” sting + color flash of that player. 1.2s easeOutBack nameplate.
- Device is a **prop**: face-down for secrets, held up for reveals, slid across the table for strokes.
- Watchers always have a **public job**: clap vote, shout a number, hold a pose, heckle the needle. Dead watchers = dead air.

**Kahoot / game-show layer**
- Countdown beep in the last 3s. Podium slam for votes. Color-blind-safe 4-color choices if you do trivia.
- Split vote bars fill with a punch, not a fade. Wrong-reveal is a record-scratch; Echo-survive is a bass drop.

**Gallery / roast moments** (Drawful, Gartic Phone, Hear Say)
- Full-screen drawing, then fake captions slam in one by one. Pause on the laugh. Then “real title” with a delayed sting.
- If you ever record a sound (Hear Say energy): 3-second take, instant playback over a dumb rooftop clip. Do not build a studio.

**Readability > particles**
- Player colors/names **always** on a persistent strip. Current phase in 6 words max (“Draw one line. Don’t lift.”).
- Juice: nameplate pop, vote-bar punch, 80–120ms hitstop on a catch, confetti on a perfect Wavelength hit, light camera punch. Never shake so hard the needle is unreadable.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. No full-screen strobe.

**Single-device input cheats**
- Prefer: tap a face, drag a needle, two-button claim/call, 12-char caption, one-finger stroke.
- Hide-screen overlay for Echo identity (“only the next player looks”). Muse counts “3, 2, 1, pass.”
- Never a keyboard for the whole room. If a prompt needs a sentence, **one** hot-seat types while Muse fills the rest of the 15s with a roast of last round.

## 5. Round-type blends (ship these as named rooftop games)

Keep each 6–12 minutes. Muse picks the next based on last round’s energy (too quiet → Wavelength; too solved → role-swap; too mean → mural).

| Blend | What happens on the roof | Win / tell |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fake Artist + vote** | Guest-book mural, one-line strokes, Echo is blind | Catch the Echo *and* they fail the title guess |
| **Suspectives survey + trial** | Opening answers become “who spilled the punch” clues; one phony | Two-vote shortlist; Echo blends or burns the fake clue |
| **Wavelength + Echo mimic** | Vibe needle; group debates; Echo secretly aims off or clones | Room scores closeness; Muse flags who “felt wrong” |
| **Spyfall Q&A + Codenames** | Shared secret (the song / the crime); questions only; one-word clues on a 4×4 party grid | Catch the Echo without naming the secret; Echo can steal by guessing it |
| **Gartic rumor mill + vote** | Write a rumor → draw it → caption it (one device, chain of 4–6) | Gallery roast, then vote which chain the Echo poisoned |
| **Resistance kitchen run + Mario Party board** | Nominate a snack run; secret sabotage; between rounds a 3-space rooftop board (star / steal / Muse chaos) | Three sabotages vs three clean runs; board is garnish, not the game |
| **Coup claims + TMP fire-escape** | Claim a tell; get called; failed players play a 20s fire-escape minigame (still on camera) | Strikes, not sit-outs; last with fewer strikes (or ghosts) |
| **Just One + Chameleon** | Table writes one-word clues about a secret party object; duplicates cancel; Echo doesn’t know the object | Guesser + table vs Echo; Echo wins by matching a clue or dodging the point |
| **Drawful dual-score + Quiplash final** | Hot-seat doodle, fake titles, then **everyone** answers the same roast prompt | Points for truth *and* bait; final is a room-wide vote |
| **One Night swap + BOTC ghost vote** | Lights-down swaps, 90s talk, point; kicked players keep one ghost vote next round | Fast identity chaos; ghosts still matter |

**Default night (4–6 players, ~45 min):** Suspectives icebreaker → mural (Fake Artist) → kitchen run (Resistance) → Wavelength palate → rumor mill gallery → one simultaneous Echo vote. Muse spends one twist token. Nobody is ever only watching.
